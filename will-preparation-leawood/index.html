What should will preparation cover?

Will preparation should start with a conversation about your family, property, and wishes, not just filling in names on a form. A lawyer will typically ask who should inherit, who you trust to manage the estate, and whether you want to nominate a guardian for minor children.

The final document should express those choices in a way that can be followed, and the lawyer should explain what the will does and does not control. Depending on your circumstances, the discussion may also cover related planning documents or steps to keep your documents current.

What will a local professional check before drafting?

A useful first review looks at more than the names of beneficiaries. Expect questions about where you live, your family relationships, the types of property you own, and whether you have existing estate documents or plans made in another state.

  • Whether your wishes are clear and workable, including backup choices if a first choice cannot serve or inherit.
  • How property is titled and whether an account or asset has a beneficiary designation.
  • Whether a marriage, divorce, new child, move, or major change in finances affects an older plan.
  • What signing and witness requirements apply to your situation under current Kansas law.

These checks help spot gaps before signing. Property ownership and beneficiary forms can affect who receives an asset, so a will should be reviewed in context rather than treated as a complete plan by itself.

What can a will handle, and what may pass another way?

A will can state who should receive property that is distributed through the will and name a personal representative to handle estate responsibilities. It can also nominate a guardian for minor children, though a court makes the final appointment based on the law and the child's circumstances.

Some property may pass outside the will, such as an account with a named beneficiary or property held with survivorship rights. A professional can compare your documents and ownership records so your instructions do not conflict. Ask specifically whether your beneficiary forms and will say what you intend.

What affects the cost and timing?

There is no single fee that applies to every will. The total can depend on the lawyer's billing method, the time needed to understand your wishes, and whether the work is limited to a will or includes review of other estate planning documents.

  • Family arrangements that require backup choices or careful wording.
  • Property in more than one state, business interests, or complex ownership.
  • Existing documents that need review or changes.
  • Whether you want additional planning documents or coordination with tax or elder law questions.
  • How quickly you need the work completed and how promptly you can provide information and approve drafts.

Timing also depends on the lawyer's schedule and how quickly decisions are made. Before work begins, ask for the fee structure, what the fee covers, what could add work, and an estimated timeline rather than relying on a general quote for a different situation.

What mistakes should you avoid?

  • Assuming a will controls every account, jointly owned asset, or beneficiary-designated policy.
  • Naming only one person for an important role, without discussing a backup.
  • Using wording or forms that do not fit your circumstances or current state requirements.
  • Leaving an old will in place after a major family or financial change without checking whether it still works.
  • Signing before confirming the required process, including who must witness the document.

A document can look complete and still leave unanswered questions. Ask the lawyer to explain any choices you are unsure about and to walk through the signing process before you sign.

Questions to Ask Checklist for Will Preparation

  • Who will meet with me, draft the will, and answer questions after the meeting?
  • What information and documents should I bring?
  • What does your fee include, and what circumstances could increase it?
  • Will you review how my property is owned and whether beneficiary designations need attention?
  • Are related documents included, or would they be a separate engagement?
  • How long does the process usually take once I provide the requested information?
  • How will you confirm the will is signed and witnessed correctly under Kansas requirements?
  • When should I review or update the will in the future?

Clear answers make it easier to compare providers. If a lawyer cannot explain the scope or likely next steps in plain language, ask for clarification before agreeing to proceed.

When should you call a will preparation professional?

Consider getting advice when you have minor children, a blended family, property in multiple states, a business, or uncertainty about who should manage your estate. It is also sensible to ask for a review after a major life change or when you have an older will that may no longer reflect your wishes.

A lawyer can help you understand choices and current requirements, but you should be prepared to share accurate information about your family and property. Bring questions as well as documents; the conversation should help you decide what work is actually needed.
